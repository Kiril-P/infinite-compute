# Game design specification

## Core loop

1. Complete a job through a short timing interaction.
2. Earn Cash.
3. Buy repeatable hardware and one-time upgrades.
4. Improve throughput, job value, and automation.
5. Encounter a new bottleneck or application.
6. Research a breakthrough that changes the loop.
7. Transform the computer and its environment into the next era.

The player should usually have a useful action available, but the game must also feel comfortable when observed rather than constantly operated.

Each Era uses a control-compression arc:

1. Perform the Era's new action.
2. Understand its cause and effect.
3. Earn basic Automation.
4. Configure that Automation through a small policy choice.
5. Compress the mastered behavior into background infrastructure.
6. Encounter the next kind of decision.

## Current Objective

The OS shell always pins one critical-path Current Objective and may show up to two optional discoveries. The Current Objective states exact requirements, live progress, and its reward. Selecting a missing requirement focuses the application where it can be satisfied. The objective changes after a meaningful action or story beat, never merely because time passed.

## Economy

### Cash

- Available from the beginning.
- Earned by manual and automated jobs.
- Spent on ordinary hardware, infrastructure, and commercial software.
- The primary measure of early purchasing power, not the ultimate score.

### Research

- Introduced during the workstation era, approximately 18-22 minutes into a baseline run.
- Earned from milestone jobs and then slowly generated by allocating spare compute.
- Spent on one-time breakthroughs, new applications, automation, and era transitions.
- Should unlock qualitatively new behavior rather than act as a second Cash bar.
- Opens a small tree of approximately 8-12 nodes with at most two branch points.
- Temporary emphasis may favor CPU, GPU, networking, active bursts, or Automation, but every node is eventually obtainable.
- A free `Recompile Kernel` respec and later named Operating Profiles keep choices reversible.

### Compute

- A capability expressed as operations per second using readable period-appropriate units.
- Determines job duration and eligibility.
- Not a spendable currency in v1.
- Derived from hardware with modifiers from memory, cooling, power, software, and specialization.

### Supporting metrics

- Temperature: safe, warm, throttled; no hardware destruction in v1.
- Power draw/capacity: exceeding supply reduces active hardware efficiency.
- Bandwidth: limits remote jobs and distributed scaling.
- Reputation/trust may appear only as a lightweight modifier tied to questionable upgrades. It is not a full economy in v1.

## Manual play

Manual interaction must reward attention without rewarding mouse abuse.

### Era 1: Clock Cycle

A cursor sweeps across a timing track. Activate when it crosses a target region to process the current job. Accuracy determines a short efficiency multiplier and audiovisual feedback. Mouse and keyboard input are equivalent.

### Era 2: Batch Queue

Several compact jobs appear with visible traits such as size, reward, and deadline. Select the best short batch; automation processes the rest. The interaction takes a few seconds and rewards basic prioritization, not calculation outside the game.

### Era 3: Thermal Window

During an overclock window, make a few timed adjustments to keep temperature in a broad efficient band. Success grants a production burst; failure merely ends the boost early.

### Era 4: Packet Route

Choose one of a few readable network paths around temporary congestion. Correct routing grants a remote-job multiplier. The optimal answer should be legible at a glance.

### Era 5: Incident Console

Occasional incidents present one symptom and a few responses. Selecting the sensible response restores or boosts production. Humor comes from corporate explanations and absurd scale, not opaque punishment.

Only the current era's primary interaction needs full v1 depth. Earlier interactions may become background automation or occasional bonus events.

## Automation philosophy

- Automate a repeated action shortly before it becomes a chore.
- Automation establishes reliable baseline income.
- Active play earns bursts, better job selection, or temporary multipliers; it does not simply click faster.
- Automation upgrades should be visible: cursors move, queues fill, fans spin, jobs complete, and applications report activity.
- Automation must never make a newly introduced mechanic feel irrelevant immediately.
- Preserve configured intent even when current hardware, power, or software cannot execute it; activate the rule automatically when capacity becomes available.
- Mature Task Manager in three readable steps: auto-run, queue policy, then operations policy.
- Every rule reports its next action or the exact reason it is waiting.

### Automation maturity

1. **Auto-run:** start the next eligible Job.
2. **Queue policy:** prioritize fastest Cash, highest value, nearest milestone, or Research; optionally preserve a Cash reserve.
3. **Operations policy:** define safe temperature and power thresholds for cooling and overclock behavior.

Late v1 unlocks a few named Operating Profiles such as Balanced, Throughput, Research, and Manual Burst. A scripting language is explicitly post-v1 and must remain optional if introduced.

## Hardware model

### Repeatable categories

| Category | Primary role | Important interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU | General throughput | Benefits from cooling and software |
| RAM | Job-size limit and batching | Prevents CPU starvation on larger jobs |
| Storage | Queue capacity and data access | Faster tiers reduce job setup time |
| GPU | Parallel and specialized jobs | Large boost for rendering, crypto, and research |
| Cooling | Safe performance ceiling | Enables sustained clocks and overclock events |
| Power | Total active capacity | Weak supply creates a transparent efficiency penalty |
| Network | Remote job value and bandwidth | Enables distributed work and announcements |
| Software | Automation and global multipliers | Adds applications and changes behaviors |

Every repeatable Hardware Category has visible authored breakpoints in addition to smooth gains. Breakpoints unlock historically flavored capabilities, change application behavior, or alter the machine visual. The next breakpoint and remaining purchases are visible beside the buy control.

### Tier gates

Motherboards, chassis, rooms, racks, and electrical service are one-time milestones. They unlock category caps and transform the visual environment. They should feel larger than buying another repeatable part.

### Compatibility

Use light interactions and bottlenecks, not socket-level simulation. A player should understand why performance is constrained from a single sentence such as “CPU waiting on memory” or “rack power capped at 80%.”

## Jobs and milestones

Jobs unlock automatically at compute and story thresholds. They should use recognizable scale comparisons and provide Cash, Research, or a one-time unlock.

Candidate progression:

- Balance a household spreadsheet
- Decode a suspiciously compressed photograph
- Encode an MP3 acquired through entirely normal channels
- Render a spinning 3D logo
- Host a personal website visited by three people and a crawler
- Run a protein-folding work unit
- Mine a fraction of a parody cryptocurrency
- Defeat a chess computer described by legal as “historically adjacent”
- Render a feature film's least important dust particle
- Process a large-language-model training batch with an alarming electricity tooltip
- Complete a climate or physics simulation
- Benchmark the first data-center supercomputer

Do not require manual compute allocation in v1. The active job is selected or unlocked; eligible automation handles the queue.

## Era Benchmarks

Every Era ends with a deterministic Benchmark that temporarily changes one known rule. The Benchmark screen states the changed rule, exact goal, current Bottleneck, permanent reward, and an exit/retry action. Failure costs seconds, never resources or hardware.

| Era | Benchmark | Temporary rule | Permanent relief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beige PC | Safe Mode Payroll | Software assistance is disabled | Auto-run the next basic Job |
| Home PC | Memory Pressure Test | Large Jobs stall when RAM cannot feed the CPU | Batch Queue policy |
| Workstation | Heat Soak Certification | Compute climbs into thermal throttling | Automatic fan curve |
| Server rack | Brownout Drill | Fixed power budget forces a visible workload priority | Power-priority policy |
| Data center | Packet Loss Audit / final integrated Benchmark | A signposted route is congested while all major systems are active | Automatic fallback and the v1 finale |

Benchmarks are the v1 equivalent of a boss or challenge: preparation and diagnosis matter more than reaction speed. They are also Storylines—completion produces an OS event and a rule-changing reward, not only Cash.

## Upgrades

Use two complementary forms:

- **Repeatable purchases:** ordinary components with increasing cost and readable production gain.
- **Named breakthroughs:** one-time upgrades that multiply a system, remove friction, unlock an application, or transform the era.

Named upgrades should carry most jokes and real-world explanations. Examples are provisional until content implementation:

- “Download More RAM”: does nothing until marketing converts outrage into a small reputation bonus.
- “Enterprise Cable”: identical electrons, better invoice.
- “Responsible Telemetry”: observes responsibility continuously.
- “AI Inside”: replaces three buttons with a slower animated button.

## Events and consequences

- Positive opportunities are common; disruptive events are initially rare and brief.
- Heat, grid, network, update, lawsuit, and PR incidents reduce efficiency temporarily rather than destroy progress.
- Questionable upgrades may improve production while slightly increasing incident frequency or reducing job value.
- Every negative state must show its cause, exact effect, and obvious recovery path.
- Events should escalate from domestic inconvenience to global absurdity.

## Interface unfolding

The game begins with Job Runner, a minimal status area, and Hardware Manager. Other applications appear only when useful:

1. Job Runner
2. Hardware Manager
3. Shop
4. Task Manager / automation
5. Cooling & Power
6. Research Lab
7. Network
8. News / announcements
9. Achievements
10. Settings

`Help Center` or `ReadMe.txt` is part of the shared OS shell rather than another economy panel. Entries appear only when their system unlocks and contain a one-sentence purpose, an immediate action, optional technical details, and a link to the relevant application. A dynamic `Why am I slow?` entry identifies the current Bottleneck.

Applications are fixed responsive panels styled as windows. Their content and shell boundaries must allow true movable windows later.

When an Era is mastered, old Jobs complete in accelerated batches, their application may collapse into a live status tile, and obsolete controls disappear. Show at least one before/after comparison—such as `4m 12s then → 0.08s now`—so compression is visible rather than implied.

## Achievements

Achievements celebrate milestones, technical references, unusual behavior, bad hardware decisions, and puns. Visible achievements may provide a small explicit global multiplier. Secret achievements for interacting with the clock, folders, or system dialogs are cosmetic only so required power never depends on a guide.

## End of v1

The final sequence should:

1. Complete the data-center build.
2. Temporarily quiet the interface and music.
3. Run the largest available benchmark/calculation.
4. Celebrate a historically enormous result.
5. Compare it with the compute required for the impossible question.
6. Display an effectively zero completion estimate.
7. Let the OS respond with understated curiosity rather than an explicit sequel advertisement.

The finale is an Operating System state, not just a modal: ordinary purchases temporarily yield, applications report that the kernel is waiting, notification noise falls away, and the display focuses on the calculation before normal control returns.

Do not reveal 42 in v1.
