# Product brief

## One-sentence pitch

Upgrade a cozy 1990s family computer into an increasingly impossible supercomputer through satisfying jobs, automation, optimization, and a fictional operating system that slowly realizes it has been asked to compute the answer to an impossible question.

## Product promise

The player should feel a complete journey from zero to one hundred:

1. **Cozy competence:** a warm CRT, a noisy hard drive, and one small job at a time.
2. **Compulsive improvement:** every purchase produces a clear visual or numerical payoff.
3. **Optimization fever:** several systems interact, but automation removes expired chores.
4. **Controlled chaos:** racks, grid power, networking, research, and announcements compete for attention.
5. **Perspective shift:** the new supercomputer is enormous by human standards and still negligible compared with the ultimate question.

## Audience and platform

- Desktop browser only for v1.
- Suitable for players who enjoy Cookie Clicker-style compounding, unfolding games, technology jokes, and light optimization.
- Technical knowledge is optional. Surface text stays accessible; tooltips may explain real concepts.
- Free personal project for now.

## Design pillars

### The computer is the protagonist

There is no player avatar. Story is visible in the machine, its room, its operating system, its applications, and the outside world's reaction.

### Every era changes what attention means

Active play begins as a simple timing task. Later, the operator makes short, high-value decisions: batching work, managing a performance window, routing traffic, or responding to an incident. Repetition is automated away.

### Real concepts, exaggerated consequences

CPU, RAM, storage, GPU, heat, electricity, and bandwidth behave recognizably. Their scale and outcomes may become absurd. Optional tooltips teach without blocking play.

### Mechanics carry the story

The game should not pause for long exposition. New applications, system notifications, job names, visual transformations, error messages, and announcements reveal the plot.

### Satire has a target

Jokes should expose perverse incentives: surveillance, subscriptions, monopolies, energy consumption, unwanted AI features, planned obsolescence, hype, crypto, and corporate euphemisms. Consequences remain light enough not to derail the incremental loop.

## V1 scope

V1 is a polished vertical slice intended to take a new player roughly 60 minutes without offline progress. It spans five presentation eras:

1. Beige family PC
2. Upgraded home PC
3. Professional workstation
4. First server rack
5. Data-center supercomputer

V1 ends after the first significant supercomputer-scale calculation. The OS estimates that this achievement represents effectively zero progress toward the impossible question.

## Explicit non-goals for v1

- Prestige or resets
- Offline progress
- Planetary, stellar, or cosmic computing
- The final answer reveal
- Multiplayer, trading, loot boxes, or rarity collection
- Movable or overlapping windows
- Mobile layout
- Backend services or accounts
- Destructive permanent failures
- Permanent mutually exclusive builds
- Exact simulation of sockets, hardware compatibility, or electrical engineering

## Success criteria

- A first-time player understands the initial action in under 20 seconds.
- The first purchase arrives within roughly 30 seconds.
- Automation arrives before the initial manual action becomes irritating.
- A meaningful unlock, visual change, joke, or decision occurs at least every two minutes during normal play.
- The same action is not demanded unchanged for more than one era.
- Hardware transformations are unmistakable without reading numbers.
- The ending feels like a climax and a promise, not a content wall.
- A player can explain the broad story without reading a separate lore screen.
