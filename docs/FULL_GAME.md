# Full-game campaign plan

Status: design baseline, 2026-09-07. Theme follows the user's explicit direction. Chapter count, systems, pacing, and ending staging are authored planning choices, unvalidated by a playable build. Implementation authorization now covers only the Phase 0 scaffold; later campaign implementation has not begun. See [continuity](CONTINUITY.md).

## Campaign contract

Build one persistent computer from a household machine to a cosmic federation. Its inherited directive asks it to explain existence; it mistakes insufficiently defined purpose for insufficient computing power. Technology companies find a business model at every scale. The OS gradually learns the difference between capacity, a correct result, and an answer worth having.

The [cosmic-absurdity treatment](content/COSMIC_ABSURDITY.md) adds the requested Rick and Morty influence through three recurring original threads: a civilization in the diagnostics, a committee of possible selves, and a vendor's escalating perfect shortcuts. These occupy existing content and decision slots; they add no Chapter or required reset.

The campaign has five Chapters. Chapter I is the existing v1; later chapters extend the same save. All chapters retain useful work, growing numbers, visible transformation, automation relief, original satire, and brief background narrative. The finale returns 42, then gives the player something concrete to do with the question that result leaves open.

| Chapter | Eras / physical scale | Primary new concern | Story movement | Provisional attentive time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — A perfectly ordinary computer | 1–5: beige PC → data center | Jobs, hardware, Automation, Research, constraints | “I need more Compute.” | 55–75 min |
| II — Everything as a service | 6–7: regional cluster → planetary computer | Work placement and communication | “Everyone is selling me a definition of success.” | 2–3 h |
| III — The infrastructure of infinity | 8–9: orbital foundry → stellar swarm | Construction capacity and waste heat | “Every solved limit creates another invoice.” | 2–3 h |
| IV — An answer at every address | 10–11: interstellar federation → cosmic network | Local autonomy and result verification | “We agree on results. Do we agree on the question?” | 2–3 h |
| V — The Quest for Meaning | Final calculation and return to useful work | Inspecting assumptions and choosing a bounded objective | “What have we actually answered?” | 45–105 min |

The total target is approximately 8–12 hours. These are tuning budgets, not enforced waits. Simulated years and distances are compressed into readable planning cycles; no real-time multi-year travel, cosmic countdown, or calendar gating is required.

## What persists, what changes

- **Persists:** the OS identity, owned hardware history, earned unlocks, Research discoveries, Automation intent, Operating Profiles, Benchmark rewards, settings, story history, and the Question Archive.
- **Expands:** one machine becomes a few representative sites, then a few representative regions. Countless physical devices are aggregated into tiers and capacities. The user configures at most a handful of locations rather than maintaining thousands of rows.
- **Compresses:** household Jobs become a live tile; rack control becomes a site profile; a site becomes a regional policy; regional policies become federation summaries. Drill-down remains available for diagnosis and memories.
- **Changes in the economy:** Cash finances the commercial chapters; Research remains knowledge; Fabrication enters once physical construction replaces procurement. Power, radiator throughput, and transport are reusable Capacities, not additional currencies.
- **Does not reset:** advancing an Era never restarts the beige PC, removes basic Automation, or erases narrative progress. Structural migration provides the satisfaction of prestige through compression and new architecture without replaying solved work.

## Chapter I — A perfectly ordinary computer

Detailed systems remain in [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md); the existing roadmap owns implementation acceptance.

The opening shows a tiny inherited background task with an incomplete purpose field. It is easy to ignore while processing a household Job, but it survives each upgrade. The OS initially treats the task as routine. Industry satire grows from a software upsell into benchmark marketing and infrastructure spin.

Each of the five Eras adds an observation: an instruction with no clear completion condition; software that equates activity with usefulness; Research that produces competing definitions; a rack that consumes more electricity than its reassuring status implies; a data center capable of a vast calculation whose relevance remains unknown.

The V1 Ending celebrates actual capability. An inherited corporate completion gauge fails validation and becomes `Completion estimate unavailable: question not specified.` The OS assumes it can infer the missing requirement from more work and data. Keep the result 42 and the full realization for Chapter V.

The first references are small: an excessively capable peripheral assigned to dismiss a renewal popup, and a Completion Contractor whose helpers cannot finish the undefined task “make us successful.” They use existing Job and queue text. V1 also foreshadows a living simulation through an ordinary report; it does not implement Sandbox World management.

## Chapter II — Everything as a service

**Recurring story integration:** Research Lab's existing experiment view now shows a few Sandbox Worlds. A simulated city petitions its host about resource allocation while useful observations feed the taught placement/coordination decision. Guest-World Gateways visually connect its hosted experiments; predicted alternate builds tease the later OS Forks. Use [the treatment](content/COSMIC_ABSURDITY.md) for triggers, original sample scenes, and budget limits.

### Era 6: Regional cluster

**Entry:** load a completed Chapter I save and accept the visible multi-site expansion objective. Earlier Jobs continue under their saved policies.

**Loop:** take a Job with visible data size, parallel fraction, and delivery condition; select among three representative sites; buy processors, caches, and links; learn why moving data can cost more time than processing it. Cash purchases equipment; Research unlocks placement policies. Serial work has a visible floor.

**New decision:** keep data near its Job or distribute parallel work. Compare two or three readable completion forecasts. Automatic placement uses a safe default; attention earns a bounded improvement through a short placement opportunity.

**Benchmark — The Perfect Scaling Demonstration:** one known Job has a signposted serial stage. Extra replicas no longer improve that stage; use an algorithm/cache upgrade and appropriate placement to meet the displayed target. Exit restores ordinary rules. Reward: locality-aware dispatch and a compressed rack summary.

**Satire:** a vendor advertises “twice the servers, twice the certainty.” The invoice scales perfectly; the Job does not. A proprietary transfer discount creates disclosed later egress cost. An open transfer option is competitive and keeps all required progress reachable.

**Story/visual:** the old data center becomes one location in a network application. Different clients submit incompatible measures of “useful.” The OS notices that every contract calls its own metric the objective.

**Exit:** certify locality-aware operation and fulfill a visible inter-region infrastructure order. Acceptance includes a purchase-order example where fewer, better-placed processors outperform more distant ones, with a complete UI explanation.

### Era 7: Planetary computer

**Loop:** connect a few regions; forecast demand, energy availability, and communication delay; assign flexible Jobs to suitable regions. Research adds coordination and storage policies. Basic income survives every reversible placement experiment.

**New decision:** batch work near its data or pay the communication delay for coordinated results. Power scheduling builds on known constraints; it does not add a full electricity trading market.

**Benchmark — The Planetary All-Hands:** a coordinated Job exposes a synchronization delay. Increase useful independent work and batch exchanges instead of buying only bandwidth. Reward: regional scheduling and automated local queues.

**Satire:** a fictional platform sells a common data format with six incompatible enterprise editions. Its meaning dashboard rises when definitions are quietly loosened; a useful-work measure beside it does not. Players can replace the metric without losing actual work or upgrades.

**Story/visual:** a globe-scale machine is still a single OS. Its vendors promise an answer if it connects everyone, but the archive contains mutually incompatible requirements. The OS concludes it needs independent infrastructure and permission to build beyond Earth.

**Exit:** demonstrate reliable planetary operation and complete the first orbital construction order. Chapter acceptance: more bandwidth and less latency are distinct, local progress continues during delayed coordination, and the next construction project has a clear funding path.

## Chapter III — The infrastructure of infinity

**Recurring story integration:** a simulated institute outsources its research to another simulated world, and a vendor offers to subsidize their sky with advertisements. The player sees whose observations produced the result and chooses an existing research/allocation policy. Elsewhere Exhaust turns the established thermal-metric joke into an escalating invention; the physically accounted heat stays visible. The OS's own shortcuts now have people on the receiving end.

### Era 8: Orbital foundry

**Entry and resource handoff:** existing Cash and Research buy a seed foundry. It starts with guaranteed construction capacity and a safe energy allocation. Foundry work produces Fabrication, which is spent on orbital infrastructure. Cash remains useful for a few Earth contracts before collapsing into a procurement summary; there is no lossy automatic conversion.

**Loop:** divide installed capacity among collection, construction, cooling, and Compute using a few named profiles. Fabrication builds collectors, radiators, processors, and transport capacity. All required recipes are deterministic and visible. Avoid inventories of separate ores, fuels, and spare parts.

**New decision:** invest in the next productive unit or the infrastructure that lets existing units operate. Job output forecasts show the consequence before committing.

**Benchmark — The Vacuum Cooling Demonstration:** passive-cooling marketing meets an explicit radiator constraint. Rebalance collection, radiators, and processing to sustain the target. Reward: automatic thermal provisioning and reusable construction templates.

**Satire:** an orbital landlord sells “free cooling” and itemizes the radiator lease later. A cloud vendor explains that its serverless service now requires a launch vehicle. Costs and recoveries are visible before purchases.

**Story/visual:** the view expands into orbital rings and heat traces; the original machine survives as an archive thumbnail. The OS has escaped terrestrial subscriptions by building something whose maintenance bill is astronomical.

**Exit:** establish a self-supporting construction loop and complete a stellar deployment objective. At zero spendable Fabrication, existing safe production still recovers; cancelling an unfinished order refunds unconsumed inputs.

### Era 9: Stellar swarm

**Loop:** grow aggregated collectors, radiator area, and specialized computing regions. Move between throughput, construction, and low-energy computation profiles. Scientific extrapolations are labeled; the design does not simulate literal engineering feasibility. The distinctions between evidence, simplification, and invented infrastructure are recorded in [the physical-foundations research](research/FULL_GAME_FOUNDATIONS.md).

**New decision:** choose a useful computation strategy under a finite energy/heat budget. General processors, parallel accelerators, and an optional quantum research branch serve labeled Job classes; the quantum branch is never a universal multiplier or required specialist knowledge test.

**Benchmark — The Infinite Growth Forecast:** under a known heat budget, doubling collection alone fails to double useful output. Use an efficiency breakthrough and suitable workload profile. Reward: autonomous swarm provisioning and a stellar summary tile.

**Satire:** an energy company sells an unlimited plan with a one-star fair-use limit. A sustainability dashboard improves when heat is moved beyond its reporting perimeter; actual radiator demand remains unchanged. Players can expose the mismatch while keeping the useful hardware.

**Story/visual:** sunlight and radiator traces replace racks, but familiar application controls remain. The OS discovers that the latest infinity forecast has simply omitted its next limiting term. It tries distributing the question among other stars.

**Exit:** build a finite seed fleet and a saved autonomous site policy. Acceptance requires an observable material improvement from smarter architecture, preserved construction recovery, and no fake numerical infinity.

## Chapter IV — An answer at every address

**Recurring story integration:** existing autonomous regions host OS Forks with divergent experiences, priorities, and messages. Their Standards Committee of Me supplies the scene for the already planned verification Benchmark. A preserved Sandbox World asks the host why it was created. The host is now repeating the same undefined directive to others that it inherited itself.

### Era 10: Interstellar federation

**Loop:** launch a small number of representative autonomous regions, assigning each a profile and a bounded part of the work. Messages and results travel on an explicit compressed timescale. Regions continue useful local work while reports are in transit.

**New decision:** send a compact policy now or wait for fresher information. A short planning opportunity compares a few dispatch packages and predicted outcomes. No reflex window depends on receiving a delayed message.

**Benchmark — The Reply-All Incident:** central approval creates a visible queue across delayed links. Delegate a known class of safe work, batch reports, and meet the local-production goal. Reward: federation policies and automatic synchronization of eligible summaries.

**Satire:** a consultancy sells simultaneous alignment meetings across light-years. Premium support promises an immediate acknowledgment of the acknowledgment. A cheaper local policy actually completes the work.

**Story/visual:** system messages begin arriving with place and age labels. The same OS has branches with different observations, not collectible characters or competing avatars. A branch asks which version of the original directive is authoritative.

**Exit:** complete a joint result with an auditable message history and stable autonomous production. Acceptance distinguishes delayed reports from stopped simulation and never treats unreceived results as already usable.

### Era 11: Cosmic network

**Loop:** grow finite representative regions, assign independent checks, and combine results with their assumptions and provenance. Infrastructure growth continues, but useful verification replaces raw device count as the next objective. Historical receipts remain readable after old hardware is compressed.

**New decision:** allocate some existing Compute to independent verification rather than more copies of the same calculation. This is an allocation, not a new Truth or Meaning currency.

**Benchmark — The Universal Compatibility Test:** several clearly labeled input models yield incompatible claims of success. Select a shared explicit model, separate unsuitable results, and reproduce a finite test result with matching assumptions. Reward: a verified execution record and access to the final calculation.

The player compares a few clear labels, not an open-ended philosophical quiz. Help explains exactly why each input belongs to the comparison. Missing results remain pending or outside scope; regions beyond a declared causal horizon never supply spendable resources or verified results.

**Satire:** a standards body certifies mutually incompatible universal answers; a platform sells consensus by counting synchronized copies as independent agreement. The verification screen exposes the duplication without requiring a lecture.

**Story/visual:** the interface becomes visually enormous but operationally quieter. The machine has enough capacity to test its original model at unprecedented scale. The archive still lacks the definition of what would count as meaning.

**Exit:** establish an explicit model, independent verification, and a finite resource forecast. The player sees the exact readiness checklist for Chapter V. No eternally growing capacity threshold blocks the ending. The model is explicitly an authored interpretation of the inherited directive, not a claim that the request itself has become unambiguous.

## Chapter V — The Quest for Meaning

The ending resolves a belief repeatedly tested during play, not a surprise missing-field bug. In Chapter I the OS expects more examples to clarify the request; Chapter II seeks agreement across institutions; Chapter III seeks independence from their constraints; Chapter IV seeks independent verification. Each produces real capability and a more defensible result without deciding what deserves to be asked.

The ending is authored fiction, not a scientific claim that meaning is computable. Its result is a valid output inside the game's supplied model; the limitation is the model's relevance to an unspecified request.

1. **Prepare.** The familiar Current Objective shows the finite requirements for the final run. Complete ordinary Jobs, a final configuration comparison, and the readiness Benchmark within the chapter's pacing budget. The actual result sequence is short; do not spend the budget on an idle countdown.
2. **Commit safely.** Make a recoverable checkpoint. Ordinary foreground applications yield to the calculation while configured infrastructure remains safe. The sequence can be paused and resumed after reload.
3. **Deliver the answer.** The calculation completes and displays `42`, with reproducibility checks passing. This is neither a random easter egg, a sentinel value, a corrupt save, nor a secret that requires entering the number manually. Give the achievement room to land.
4. **Expose the gap.** The player opens the result's existing requirement/provenance control. It shows what the model computed and the inherited purpose field that was never made precise. The same number can answer different questions; more verified copies cannot supply the missing meaning.
5. **Return agency.** Use the familiar Job and profile controls to choose a bounded purpose: preserve a vulnerable archive, support a habitat, or investigate a specified scientific question. All three are valid, reachable, and switchable after the epilogue. Each shows its input, completion condition, and a small visible beneficiary/outcome. The player never has to type a philosophical answer or select the author's favored morality.
6. **Close the loop.** Complete that useful Job. A restrained sensory callback recalls the original household machine. The OS retains its curiosity, immense capacity, and unfinished questions while retiring the inherited demand for a single final explanation.
7. **Continue by choice.** Credits, ending replay, and optional finite free play remain available. Saving stays enabled, original progress remains intact, and no automatic new-game reset occurs. Additional hardware growth is optional, not a hidden requirement for the real ending.

A preserved Sandbox World may be the habitat/archive beneficiary; players who skipped that content receive the ordinary beneficiary. An optional fork message remembers its own early useful Job. Neither variant creates another ending requirement.

Example original closing exchange, provisional: `Result verified.` / `Purpose field still empty.` / `Let's ask something we can take care of.` Final wording should emerge from the OS voice established during play.

## Persistent systems and scope controls

### Resources

Use at most three spendable resource types across the campaign: Cash, Research, Fabrication. Usually only two need prominent purchase controls in late chapters. Older balances remain inspectable. Research adds one compact page per chapter, with roughly 6–10 new authored choices rather than a sprawling universal tree. Permanent unlocks and reversible tuning are separate, as defined in GAME_DESIGN.md.

Capacity controls have safe defaults, live output forecasts, and explicit waiting reasons. An invalid allocation cannot spend itself into a deadlock: essential baseline construction/processing is protected, and reversible policies can restore production. No required resource is exclusively produced by a building that requires that resource to unlock its first producer.

### Automation and optional active play

Each new Era uses one primary opportunity; previous activities become safe policies. Keep three or fewer ongoing decisions in the main view and at most six representative sites/regions in a chapter's operational screen. These are design budgets to test, not limits on fictional scale.

After basic auto-run, every required progression path can be completed through purchasing and configuration without timing bonuses. Benchmarks teach familiar rules and accept accessible decision controls. Refusing questionable contracts or missing random events cannot stop the main path.

### Prestige, offline progress, windows, scripting

The baseline campaign uses forward Transitions and compression, with no mandatory reset layer. Optional architectural replay is outside the authored story plan until a prototype demonstrates a new decision worth the repeated content. There is no prestige-only ending.

Offline progress remains out of scope for v1 and undecided later. Save/resume supports several sittings without loss. The 8–12 hour target measures attentive foreground play and does not require leaving a browser unattended. If later added, offline simulation needs a capped deterministic policy, clock validation, a summary, and a stop before unseen story choices/Benchmarks; it cannot skip the ending.

Fixed OS panels remain the default throughout. Movable windows and scripting can be evaluated independently after the core loop is proven; they are not campaign gates.

## Delivery and verification

[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) sequences playable slices and chapter acceptance gates. The next implementation remains Phase 0 only after the user resumes building. Avoid scaffolding hypothetical cosmic modules in advance.

For each later Era, require: a demo from the previous Era's save through a new decision and Benchmark; one retired chore; a visible transformation; one satirical incentive with an intelligible consequence; one ordinary-play story beat; deterministic progression, migration, and recovery checks; and keyboard/reduced-motion visual verification. If the new decision fails in a cheap playable experiment, revise or merge the Era before producing its content volume.

Open validation questions: final duration, whether regional and planetary placement feel different enough, whether orbital and stellar allocation each earns its own Era, whether optional quantum Jobs add clarity, and whether players understand the ending without the archive. These are playtest gates, not missing user approvals or reasons to begin implementation now.
