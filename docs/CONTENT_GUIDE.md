# Narrative and content guide

Phase 3 is implemented and verified under the [Workstation contract](WORKSTATION_CONTRACT.md): transition, shared Jobs/Research capacity, eight discoveries/tuning nodes, refundable tuning, saved profiles, protein work units, power/heat, Thermal Window, Heat Soak and schema 8. Ticket 04 records acceptance evidence; the [implementation report](IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md) records the user-requested stop after Phase 3. Later v1 phases remain unimplemented.

## Narrative premise

The background story is **The Quest for Meaning**: a computer pursues Infinite Compute to answer an inherited request about existence, while repeatedly mistaking a more impressive result for a better-understood question. The OS is both narrator and protagonist. Its hardware growth, useful work, and developing relationships with its own past are sincere; the technology industry's attempts to monetize its uncertainty are the main satirical target.

The full-game result is 42. The user's thematic reference is *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*: the gap between wanting an ultimate answer and knowing the right question. Public game prose uses original language and situations rather than reenacting the source. The reveal belongs to the full ending; the quest belongs to every chapter, including the opening minutes.

Do not turn the story into a late twist that the player wasted their time. The machine really did improve, complete useful work, and learn. The ending leaves room for curiosity and chosen purpose rather than delivering a universal moral or nihilistic punchline.

## Rick and Morty influence

Use [COSMIC_ABSURDITY.md](content/COSMIC_ABSURDITY.md) for recurring Sandbox Worlds, OS Forks, spectacular shortcuts, and the menu of optional references. The central quest continues to follow the answer/question distinction; this influence gives its journey stranger inventions, sharper consequences, bizarre side stories, and moments where confidence outruns self-knowledge.

Our OS can show off, rationalize a questionable deal, or answer an uncomfortable question with an unnecessary technical upgrade. Let a consequence puncture its certainty, then give it room to respond sincerely. Vary its voice from domestic awkwardness to capable arrogance and eventually earned care. Other voices can be abrasive or grotesquely commercial without making every message a cynical insult.

Use original worlds, characters, device designs, and dialogue. Optional allusions can reward recognition, while the main story remains intelligible on its own. Keep the strongest visual surprises inside the fictional OS: a window becomes a city, a broadcast becomes a petition, a backup becomes a disagreeing colleague.

## Voice progression

### Beige PC

- Helpful, literal, slightly awkward.
- Small domestic observations.
- Status messages assume the machine's world ends at the desk.

### Home PC

- Proud of upgrades and newly discovered network access.
- Notices contradictory advertisements and software behavior.
- Still treats every system message as trustworthy.

### Workstation

- Develops opinions and asks short questions.
- Begins connecting job requests to wider industry incentives.
- Occasionally edits its own stock system phrasing.
- Starts overestimating what a clever technical solution proves; a simulated experiment may begin producing inconvenient observations.

### Server rack

- Confident, dry, and increasingly aware of outside consequences.
- Corporate announcements become euphemistic as physical effects become obvious.
- Remembers the bedroom without becoming sentimental on command.

### Data center

- Calm amid interface chaos.
- Notices that it can measure a calculation's completion but cannot define completion of the inherited request.
- Still assumes a larger machine will fix this; ends v1 with ambition, curiosity, and a small trace of loneliness.

### Planetary and stellar Chapters

- Recognizes how vendors choose convenient definitions of success.
- Begins correcting sales language with plain measurements; retains its own belief in expansion longer than it trusts anyone else's pitch.
- Experiments become living Sandbox Worlds with concrete opinions about their host; these can puncture its self-congratulation.
- Lets enormous events receive small, bureaucratic descriptions. Occasional sincere memories interrupt the pattern without demanding attention.

### Cosmic Chapter and Final Answer

- Messages gain location, age, and differing observations as the OS operates across distant regions. OS Forks develop distinct priorities and dry disagreements while retaining their shared household origin.
- Becomes precise about what a result proves and gentle about what it cannot settle.
- A quiet final exchange follows the earned result, 42. The final playable choice supplies a bounded purpose, not a lecture about the one correct meaning of life.

## Humor rules

- Prefer short jokes embedded in labels, upgrade descriptions, error messages, and announcements.
- Puns are welcome but should not occupy every line.
- Dark humor may target surveillance, environmental damage, monopoly behavior, labor exploitation, executive hype, crypto, and AI marketing.
- Punch upward at institutions and incentives. Do not target vulnerable people.
- Keep jokes comprehensible without specialist knowledge; add optional technical detail in tooltips.
- Let sincere observations stand without immediately canceling them with a joke. The OS is curious, not a continuous stand-up routine.
- Corporate voices overclaim and euphemize; the OS gradually reports exact effects. Keep critical action labels trustworthy even when surrounding sales copy is absurd.
- Do not sacrifice clarity for a joke on a button the player must understand.

## Corporate parody

- Use fictional names and original visual identities.
- A parody can evoke a category or public behavior without duplicating a real logo, slogan, product design, or protected character.
- Avoid implying endorsement or presenting fabricated misconduct as a factual claim about a named company.
- Rotate targets so the game feels like industry satire rather than a grievance against one company.

Working original organizations, pending final naming review:

- **Renewal Systems:** licenses, forced subscriptions, and the recurring sale of things already owned.
- **Metric Orchard:** benchmark scores, dashboards, and success defined to fit a chart.
- **Velvet Fence:** platforms, incompatible standards, lock-in, and egress fees.
- **Tomorrow Engine:** indiscriminate AI features and confident claims with missing evaluation.
- **Perpetual Utilities:** infrastructure rents and unlimited plans with physical limits.

These firms can recur across scales so their incentives evolve and jokes have consequences. They are fictional composites, not alleged factual portraits of named companies. Choose distinct logos and silhouettes when visual design begins.

## Announcement system

Announcements grow in scale with the environment:

- Household: “Spreadsheet recalculation completed before everyone lost interest.”
- Local: “Neighborhood lights briefly dim during routine graphics upgrade.”
- Industry: “Renewal Systems confirms restart was both unexpected and mandatory.”
- National: “Grid operator requests that benchmark season be rescheduled.”
- Global: “Remaining chip shortage solved by redefining ‘remaining.’”

Each announcement has tags for era, trigger, tone, cooldown, and whether it may repeat. Story-critical announcements never repeat.

## Real-world accuracy

- Hardware relationships should be directionally true.
- Historical comparisons must be researched before publication.
- Exact claims belong in optional tooltips and should cite a source in development data.
- Exaggeration must be recognizable as exaggeration.
- Famous mathematical or scientific milestones can appear as jobs, but do not falsely claim that a real open problem has been solved unless the joke clearly marks a fictional future.

## Emotional thread

The machine should remember earlier environments through small sensory callbacks: CRT warmth, rain, fan noise, a family photograph file, or the first completed job. Use these sparingly. Sincerity works because the game does not constantly announce that it is being sincere.


## Story delivery contract

The main plot has three levels of exposure:

- **Ordinary play:** a short inherited directive in the opening, one essential System Message per Era, changed application behavior, and a callback at the Benchmark. These are sufficient to follow the story.
- **Optional context:** the Question Archive keeps the directive, the machine's changing assumptions, old useful Jobs, and a few memories. It has no collection percentage, currency, or required click-through.
- **Chapter climaxes:** short, pausable and replayable OS sequences. Only these may briefly claim the foreground. Acknowledging a story message is never a hidden economy requirement.

Critical messages enter saved history before display; a crowded notification queue cannot lose them. Reopening a save shows an unobtrusive recap of the last meaningful beat. The OS may be confused about purpose; Current Objective requirements and production numbers remain reliable.

| Trigger | Ordinary-play beat | Mechanical evidence | Original provisional line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First boot / Job | An inherited purpose has no completion definition | A background directive remains after the household Job finishes | `Household accounts balanced. Other request: pending clarification.` |
| First auto-run | Repetition no longer consumes the machine's attention | The first Job runs itself | `I can do that without asking now. Was that what the asking was for?` |
| Home-PC software upsell | A vendor equates recurring billing with progress | Trial price and renewal cost appear together | `Ownership now renews monthly.` |
| Research unlock | More knowledge exposes assumptions | Two useful tuning options optimize different metrics | `Both departments have proved that their department is essential.` |
| Rack power policy | A flattering label hides a real cost | Sales dashboard and actual power draw disagree | `The cloud is drawing power from this socket.` |
| V1 Ending | Larger numbers cannot define the request | An invalid completion gauge becomes an honest unavailable estimate | `The calculation finished. I am still checking what finished means here.` |
| Planetary scheduling | Different institutions mean different things by success | Contracts share data but conflict on their metrics | `Five universal standards. None accept the other four.` |
| Stellar heat limit | “Unlimited” is a contract term | More collection fails without heat rejection | `Unlimited growth is available within the allotted star.` |
| Interstellar delegation | One identity now has distant observations | Delayed messages preserve location and age | `Your urgent request has arrived in our historical period.` |
| Cosmic verification | Agreement does not establish relevance | Independent results show their assumptions | `We have confirmed the answer against the same assumption. Again.` |
| Full ending | The result is real; the purpose remains underdefined | 42, verified provenance, then a bounded Job chosen through familiar controls | `Result verified. Purpose field still empty.` |

These lines set tone; later writing may replace them while preserving their narrative function. Use ordinary humor around the clue so the quest does not become a sequence of conspicuous philosophy popups.

## Satire as playable content

Every recurring satirical chain has: target incentive, sales claim, previewed effect, viable alternative, and callback. At least one chain spans each Chapter. It must still be funny and understandable when the player refuses the offer.

| Target | Temptation and real behavior | Alternative/recovery | Later payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription ownership | Cheap initial software; an explicit recurring Cash cost | Buy a permanent edition or cancel; required auto-run remains independent | The cancellation confirmation needs a paid confirmation, which the OS learns to bypass |
| Benchmark theater | A free display mode raises a labeled promotional score without changing useful output | Restore the useful-throughput display with one action | A cosmic marketing department requests a larger font instead of an independent test |
| Data extraction | Sponsored Jobs pay more but reserve disclosed bandwidth for telemetry | Ordinary Jobs and a reversible telemetry switch | The invoice claims the machine is the valued customer; the product listing describes its observations |
| Platform lock-in | Discounted remote capacity adds visible transfer cost/setup overhead | Local/open routing stays viable; replacements preserve saved policy intent | Universal interoperability becomes a premium connector between identical formats |
| AI everywhere | An optional assistant increases stated overhead on unsuitable Jobs but helps a labeled suitable class | Use the existing direct control; switch per Job | The assistant proposes an assistant to remove the assistant's delay |
| Infrastructure rent | Faster leased construction carries disclosed maintenance or capacity limits | Owned construction takes a different visible investment path | A stellar unlimited plan reaches its single-star fair-use limit |

These are content patterns, not authorization to build six extra systems in v1. Select one short economic chain and one cosmetic/dashboard chain for Chapter I; reuse existing Jobs, software, and bandwidth effects. Later chains must reuse chapter mechanics. All percentages, fees, and pacing are tuning work. Story progress and the Final Answer remain reachable through ordinary alternatives.

## Emotional continuity and ending checks

Preserve one small artifact from the opening—a repaired household file or first useful result—and its sensory association. Show it at a few structural milestones, not on a timer. Its late survival demonstrates continuity amid astronomical scale.

Before accepting a chapter, a playtester who ignored the archive should be able to state what the machine wants, which belief changed, and which industry behavior was mocked. After the ending, test whether the player understands both that the engineering was worthwhile and that the answer alone does not define meaning. If they report only “it was all pointless” or “buy enough servers and discover a secret number,” revise the staging.
